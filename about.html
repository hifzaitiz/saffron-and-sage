About us

Three generations, one notebook

We have changed the tables, the roof and the crockery. The recipes have stayed exactly where they were.

1998

It started with six tables

Our grandfather, Ghulam Sarwar, opened a six-table dhaba on Fort Road with one karahi, one tandoor and a notebook of recipes he had learned from his mother in Amritsar.

He had one rule that we still repeat to every new cook: if you would not serve it to your own family, throw it away. It is an expensive rule. We keep it anyway.

Today the dhaba is a forty-cover restaurant with a rooftop, but the kitchen still runs on that notebook — now photocopied, laminated and slightly stained with turmeric.

What we believe

Four rules we do not break

01

Buy small, buy daily

We shop every morning at Akbari Mandi. Nothing sits in a freezer waiting to become tomorrow's special.

02

No shortcuts on time

Nihari takes eight hours, haleem takes six. We will run out before we serve you a rushed pot.

03

Colour comes from food

No artificial colouring in any dish. If the curry is red, it is red because of Kashmiri chilli.

04

Feed everyone

Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free plates sit on the main menu, not on a sad separate card.

How we got here

A slow twenty-eight years

  1. 1998

    The dhaba opens

    Six tables, one tandoor, and a menu written on a chalkboard that had to be wiped clean every time we ran out of something.

  2. 2007

    The tandoor doubles

    Demand for roghni naan outgrew a single oven, so we knocked through the back wall and built a second one.

  3. 2015

    The rooftop

    We took over the floor above and opened twenty tables under open sky, facing the Badshahi Mosque.

  4. 2026

    Still the same notebook

    Third generation in the kitchen, second tandoor still going, and the recipe for nihari still unchanged.
